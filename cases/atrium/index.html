An introduction

A reading room
for the AI-augmented mind.

Atrium is a research assistant for scholars, journalists, and serious readers. It does not summarize for you. It does not think for you. It reads with you — surfaces the citations, finds the contradictions, holds the margin notes.

The problem with the current generation of AI research tools is that they have inherited the chatbot interface — a paradigm wholly unsuited to the work of inquiry. A reader does not converse with their sources. They annotate them. They cross-reference. They doubt. The interface for scholarly work must be a reading room, not a help desk.

Atrium is therefore organised around the long text and its margins. The model reads alongside you, citations appear inline, and contested claims surface in the marginalia rather than collapsing into a confident sentence. You retain the labour of judgment, which is the labour of thought.1

What follows is a working demonstration. Query the reading room with one of three prepared topics. Observe how the system constructs its argument — and how it shows its work.

1 On the moral economy of research interfaces, see Wineburg's Why Learn History, ch. 4. The argument extends naturally to machine-mediated inquiry.

Our editorial principles.

I. The text is sovereign.

Atrium will not summarise away a difficult passage. The reader's encounter with the original is the encounter that matters. Our role is to make that encounter easier to enter, not to replace it.

II. Citations are not decoration.

Every claim Atrium surfaces is anchored to its source. We treat the reading list as the product, not the answer. A scholar should be able to verify any claim within three clicks.

III. Dissent is preserved.

Where a literature contains genuine disagreement, Atrium will show you the disagreement — in the margin, not the body. We do not collapse contested fields into false consensus.

Atrium Research is set in Crimson Pro and Cormorant Garamond. The reading room runs on a private model fine-tuned on the JSTOR, Library of Congress, and Internet Archive corpora. No reader query is retained beyond the session.
