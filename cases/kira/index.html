Kira Ito.

ML engineer in Tokyo. Currently with Sakana AI.

I work on the parts of language models that decide what to attend to and when to stop. Before Sakana I spent three years at Preferred Networks, and before that I read mathematics at the University of Tokyo.

I write here irregularly. I read more than I publish. If our work overlaps, I'd be glad to hear from you.

Now

  • The idea: most agent frameworks treat the context window like a stack. We're testing whether a learned router — trained on the same trajectories the agent ran — can decide which past observations to keep verbatim, which to compress, and which to forget. Early results suggest the gains compound on tasks longer than ~40 steps. Paper in draft.

  • About thirty of us meet at a small studio in Yanaka. One paper a week, chosen by whoever volunteers. No slides, no demos — just the paper and an hour of arguing about it in a mix of Japanese and English. We had a good session on the Mamba follow-ups last month. Mail me if you want the calendar invite.

  • It is the right book to be reading while building a translation tool, and the right book to be reading while not building one. Selected work

  1. 2025 Routing what to remember: learned context selection for long-horizon agents. NeurIPS · main track

    With M. Aoki and D. Park. We introduce a small learned policy that sits inside the agent loop and decides, per step, which prior observations to keep, compress, or drop. On four long-horizon benchmarks the router matches a 4× larger context window at a fraction of the cost.

    → paper

  2. 2025 Evolving optimisers — what a year of Sakana taught me. JaxCon Tokyo · invited talk

    A practitioner's account of using evolutionary search to discover optimiser updates that beat hand-tuned baselines on three internal workloads. The honest version, including the months where nothing worked.

    → talk

  3. 2024 What we actually learned from preference data. Essay · personal site

    A long blog post arguing that most public conversations about RLHF conflate three different things — annotator preference, model preference, and downstream task quality — and that the resulting confusion has cost the field at least a year.

    → essay

  4. 2024 Yakumo — a translation tool for Japanese literary prose. Side project · open source

    A small CLI tool that wraps a fine-tuned model with the kind of editorial scaffolding human translators actually want: ruby gloss for kanji, a panel for register choices, a sidecar of source ambiguities flagged but not resolved. Used quietly by a handful of translators I know.

    → github

  5. 2023 On apprenticeship in machine learning. Asterisk Magazine · issue 06

    A short essay about what we have lost by professionalising ML research training, and what working as a junior next to an experienced engineer can still do that no course or paper can.

    → essay
