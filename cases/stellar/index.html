Stellar backs companies where artificial intelligence is the substrate, not the feature. We invest at the moment of first conviction — when a founder has a thesis and a prototype, and the next twelve months will be decided by clarity of thought rather than scale of capital.

One check per company. $250,000. No follow-ons, no pro-rata, no board seats. We do not lead, we do not co-lead, we do not signal. We arrive early, write a small cheque, and stay out of the way. The decision belongs to one person and is made within seven days of the first conversation.

The fund deploys patient capital from a small group of operating LPs who built and sold AI companies themselves. Founders we back receive a single point of contact, a private channel, and the conviction of someone whose name is on the document.

— D. V.

How we decide

A short, legible process.

If we are not the right fund, you will hear so quickly. We hold the no in as much regard as the yes.

  1. I.

    Initial read · within 24 hours

    You send three sentences and a memo or a working demo. One person reads it. If there is a thread to pull, we ask for a conversation. If there is not, we say so — with reasons, not platitudes.

  2. II.

    Conversation · sixty minutes

    One call. No second-round panel, no junior diligence, no warm-intro pageantry. We talk about the substrate, the market, and the next six months. We listen more than we speak.

  3. III.

    Decision · within seven days

    You receive a written decision from the partner. If it is a yes, a SAFE is countersigned and the wire moves within forty-eight hours. If it is a no, you receive a letter saying why, and a few names of funds better suited than ours.

About the partner

Daniel Voss.

Before Stellar, Daniel was an early operator at Helix Labs — where he led the inference-systems group through the company's acquisition by a large American search incumbent. Prior to Helix he founded two companies: Marginalia (acquired, 2019), a research-tooling business; and Pierline (wound down, 2014), an early attempt at structured-data extraction from regulatory filings.

He spent the years between operating roles in machine-learning research, first at a London university and then at a national lab. He writes about model interpretability under his own name and about company-building under the Stellar masthead. He lives between London and Lisbon, and reads more than he meets.
